# GAIN CONTROL — working notes for Claude

A Sierra-style point-and-click comedy murder mystery set in a university neuroscience department. Story and design live in `docs/STORY.md`. Read it before writing any scene, dialogue or puzzle.

## The visual language is locked

Every scene, character, object, portrait and UI element follows the line/pose/number language. The canonical references are:

- `game/` — **the game.** A multi-room engine running Acts I and II. New rooms, items and dialogue go here (see below).
- `prototype/room-101/` — the original single-file scene: room, walking, hotspots, HUD, portraits, day/night. **Match this.**
- `prototype/style-sheet/` — Style Sheet 02: characters, objects, isometric and perspective tiles, the stroke alphabet, palette.
- `docs/STORY.md` → "Visual direction" — the eight rules, palette and character color keys.

Don't introduce other looks: no gradients, glows on everything, filled shapes, textures, raster art, or generic UI fonts in the scene.

### Rules (short form)
1. **Two line weights**, drawn at native resolution and scaled up: a 1.5 line for objects and a 0.75 hairline (`.hl`) for grids, guides and interior detail. Room scenes use a 640×360 native canvas, and tiles are 320×200.
2. **Almost no fill.** Closed shapes are filled black (`.k`) so lines overlap cleanly. Color fills only for things that are *on* (LEDs, pupils, the NRT insignia, Morris's implant, a lit cube) and for solid **status chips** with black lettering.
3. **Color = material.** Use stroke classes `sW sR sB sG sK sA sV sN sD sM` (and `sX` for the always-red EXIT), never hard-coded hex, so day/night palettes switch through CSS variables.
4. **Open contours.** Faces are a jaw, a hairline and a few marks. Skin is a pink line, never a filled face.
5. **Poses, not tweens** (Game & Watch / Pocket Operator). Animation swaps pre-drawn poses in place. Characters walk in discrete steps (one pose change and position step every ~120 ms). Thrown or falling objects jump between fixed positions.
6. **Motion as drawn glyphs:** `((( )))` vibration arcs, speed dashes, sparks, drips.
7. **3D as wireframe:** exploded isometric diagrams for clue close-ups, isometric cubes for maps, perspective grids for corridors.
8. **Type:** labels, numbers, signs and HUD readouts use the condensed rounded **stroke alphabet** (`seg()` / `chip()` / `readout()` in the prototypes): big thin numbers with tiny labels above. **Dialogue and narration** use *Saira Condensed*, all caps, large (about 3cqw in conversations and 2.5cqw in narration boxes), with each line colored in its **speaker's color** and a solid speaker chip.

### Proportions and staging
- One-point perspective rooms, stage-like and front-facing, with a hairline perspective grid on the floor.
- Walking characters scale by depth (`kAtY(y) × 0.72` in Room 101). Everything else in the room is sized against them: a chair back is about half a standing figure's height, and a seated head matches a standing head. Preston at the lectern uses the same scale for his depth.
- **Unimportant things stay simple.** Anonymous audience members are just chair backs. Only story-relevant people show a head (and a single identifying mark).
- Atmosphere is drawn in STRUCT/DIM; hotspots get full color; a few colored objects are red herrings.

### Portraits (Gabriel Knight)
- Portraits are the same drawings cropped and enlarged, with portrait-only stroke widths so lines keep the same apparent weight.
- Every portrait needs `eyes-open` / `eyes-closed` and `mouth-a` / `mouth-b` poses. Preston also needs `normal-only` / `blank-only` (the Gain Controller blank-out: pinpoint pupils, earbuds lit ON-green with `((( )))`, faint scanlines).
- A character isn't named on screen until Nell has learned who they are (Iris is "THE OLD WOMAN" in Act I).

### Interaction: the chooser (no verb bar)
- **Click the floor: walk.** There is no verb bar and no verb cycling.
- **Click a hotspot:** a reticle lands on the spot, the object's name appears as a solid INK chip, and an arc of round glyph buttons fans out (above the click, or below near the top of the screen). It pops in as two poses.
- **Only the verbs that apply appear**, with object-specific labels (pizza: LOOK / TAKE SLICE; robe: LOOK / PHOTOGRAPH; Preston: LOOK / TALK / SHAKE HAND). Define them per hotspot and per shift (`HS[id][mode]` plus `LABELS[mode][id]`).
- **Inventory items** use the same chooser: LOOK, USE ON (the cursor then carries the item; the HUD shows a USE X ON chip; responses live in `WITH[hotspot][item]`), plus item-specific verbs like PLAY on the recorder once it has caught something.
- Look happens from where you stand; talk and use walk to the hotspot's stand point first. Right-click or Esc cancels.

### Science on screen
Real neuroscience, drawn correctly and simply. Laminar fMRI is shown as a close-up of folded cortex: the pial surface, six layer lines following the gyrus/sulcus, the white-matter boundary, and a depth probe. Never as concentric rings.

## The game (`game/`)
- `index.html` holds the CSS (palettes, pose classes, dialogue styles) and loads `js/main.js` as an ES module. No build step, but it must be served over HTTP.
- `js/gfx.js`: stroke alphabet (`seg`, `chip`), glyph icons, `perspective()`, `shell()`, `sideDoor()`. `js/figures.js`: walking figures and props. `js/portraits.js`: talking portraits.
- `js/rooms/<room>.js`: each room exports `{ id, name, pp, floor, entries, draw(G) → { back, sortables }, stand, labels }`. It draws itself from state (`G.mode`, `G.flags`), marks hotspots with `data-hs`, and puts depth-sorted things in `sortables`.
- `js/story/actN.js`: one file per act, holding all its words and logic: `HS[room][id][key]` (look/talk/use/go plus score), `WITH["room.id"][item]`, `CONVOS` (lines plus `choice` blocks), `check(api)` for triggers, and `start(api)`, `TITLE`, `END`. `ITEMS` lives in act1.js, and later acts add to it. A response is a string, `"WHO|line"`, `"c:convo"`, or `fn(api)` (which may be async).
- **Hotspot key:** `"morris"` when Morris is the active character, otherwise `G.mode` (`"day"`, `"night"` or `"morning"`). `go` may be `"room@entry"`, an object of several exits (`{ down: …, up: … }`, one chooser button each), a refusal string, or a function. The entry `"vent"` lands on the room's `stand.vent`.
- **Characters:** `G.char` is the active one; `G.cast` lists who can be switched to (HUD name chips or Tab); `G.chars` keeps the inactive ones' rooms and positions, and an idle character in the same room is drawn automatically. Each character has its own inventory (`G.inv.nell`, `.gus`, `.morris`).
- `js/minigames/`: `keypad.js` (the Impact Lab keypad), `fogneuro.js` (Preston's paper mill, an HTML overlay in `#panel`), `stairwell.js` (THE FOG; its result is shown in the lift cutscene, `lift.js`).
- **One interface everywhere.** Any text, card or clue close-up goes on with a click anywhere, Enter or Space (`advanceKey`, `moreMark` in main.js; a box that closes on pointerdown also swallows the click after it). Menus take number keys and Esc. Every minigame exits through `minigames/exit.js`: CLOSE (Esc) until done, then DONE (Enter); spoken lines inside minigames use the engine's chip-and-caps look (the ONE TEXT BOX block at the end of css/game.css). Minigames end without scorecards: results are told in the story (THE FOG's in the lift's readout, THE REPLICATION's as a write-up readout).
- `js/main.js`: the engine. State, chooser, stepped walking, character switching, conversations, cards, clue readouts, choice menus, death and Revise & Resubmit (a snapshot before every action), act transitions (`startAct`, `endAct`, a title-screen chapter select, progress in localStorage), and ambient pose clocks. `window.GC` is exposed for scripted tests. Test hashes: `#night` (Act I night), `#act2`, `#act2night`, `#act3`, `#act3night`, `#act4`, `#act4night`, `#act5` (Friday morning, which opens Act V; `#act4morning` is an alias), `#act5defense`, `#act5night`, `#credits` (straight to the end credits), `#accepted` (alias `#ending`: the best ending, from the crossref epilogue through the montage, coda and credits), `#major` (the MAJOR REVISIONS ending), `#inducted` (said yes: SELLOUT. on the LED panel, which glitches as the screen fills with R01s, chairs and keynotes; the credits end with an LED band, your punishment: ChairQuest), `#deskreject` (asked to think about it: DESK REJECT; the credits end on Advisor Quest).
- `api.mini(fn, opts, cls)` runs a full-screen minigame `fn(el, opts) → Promise` in `#panel` (`cls` adds layout classes such as `glmwrap` or `nodmode`). `api.pose(group, i)` sets a pose group by hand. `check()` may return any response (a string, `"WHO|line"` or `"c:convo"`).
- `js/minigames/`: also `ceiling.js` (THE CEILING: Table 1's correlations above the noise ceiling), `overlay.js` (Figure 3b/3d), `nod.js` (THE NOD, a 2-back over faculty motions), `glm.js` (THE REPLICATION). `js/cast/` holds the later acts' characters and portraits (act3a/b, act4/act4b, act5a/b). Acts IV–V add `metamer.js`, `darkbooth.js` (THE DARK: trace photons from the earbuds, dark adaptation, averted vision), `hours.js`, `lasagna.js` (THE LASAGNA), `stairwell.js` (THE FOG, now callable), `echo.js` (THE REHEARSAL / THE INDUCTION), `defense.js`, `breakers.js`, `reels.js`, `supports.js`, `crossref.js`, and the coda (`story/coda.js`). Iris is playable in Act V (`G.cast` nell, iris, morris). Act V's `END` is filled in by the ending reached (accepted, major, inducted, deskreject).
- **The perception console** (`js/console/`, also standalone at `console.html`): the dark booth's computer, opened with the booth's CONSOLE verb in Acts I–IV (`console/open.js`). It sits in a line-art CRT (zooms in from the room, warms up, switches off on close). In the game, the first open per page visit shows a notice (AN EXTRA, NOT A CLUE: nothing here is part of the mystery). Every module opens on an instruction screen from its `instructions: { lines, keys }`. The menu opens on VISION BASICS (`basics.js`: a SCREEN SIZE tile, a bank card matched on screen plus the viewing distance, gives real degrees via `ppdOf(ctx)`; QUEST staircases (`quest()`); acuity with the tumbling E, letter contrast sensitivity, orientation, spatial frequency and speed discrimination, the dipper, Lu & Dosher's equivalent noise with a perceptual-template-model fit, band-pass noise textures re-made from the pSF Toolbox's recipe (`bandNoise()`, an FFT in JS) for sensitivity across bands and one block of the pSFT scan, temporal (de Lange) sensitivity, Weber's law for light, stereoacuity with random dots via the rivalry viewing setup, a D-15-style hue arrangement, the Amsler grid, line bisection and the tilt aftereffect), then AMBIGUOUS figures (`demos.js`, including the spinning dancer), a RIVALRY shelf (`rivalry.js`: a VIEWING SETUP tile for red/green anaglyph glasses, with crosstalk check and eye balance, or a side-by-side stereoscope, with fusion frames, nonius and a one-mirror flip, kept in localStorage; `dichoptic(ctx)` draws each eye into its own 460 px canvas and composes them; Blake and Brascamp replications: dominance statistics, Levelt's propositions, suppression depth, adaptation during suppression, size and piecemeal rivalry, travelling waves, interocular grouping, flicker-and-swap, intermittent presentation, flash suppression/facilitation, breaking CFS), nine ILLUSIONS in `demos2.js` (Kanizsa, scintillating grids, motion-induced blindness, Troxler, lilac chaser, colour afterimage, stepping feet, peripheral drift, Ebbinghaus), Carrasco, Ling & Read 2004 (`carrasco.js`), five more classics in `classics2.js` (Campbell & Robson 1968 contrast sensitivity, Posner 1980 cueing, Cooper & Shepard 1973 mental rotation, Bouma 1970 crowding, Halberda et al. 2008 number sense), and ports of the PS 327 MATLAB labs: `lines.js` (01 horizontal–vertical, 02 Müller-Lyer by estimation, adjustment or PSE), `search.js` (03), `lightness.js` (04 Adelson's corrugated plaid) and `mae.js` (05). The library (70 modules) adds `lib_attention.js` (ATTENTION, ACTION), `lib_memory.js` (MEMORY, LEARNING), `lib_vision.js` (FORM, MOTION, NUMBER) and `lib_adjust.js` (ILLUSIONS, LIGHT & COLOUR, HEARING, TIME): classic paradigms chosen to work on uncalibrated screens (relative measures only). The menu is a shelf browser built from `GROUPS` in `modules.js`; `CONSOLE_REFS()` feeds the credits. A module is `{ id, group, tag, title, sub, blurb, ref (string or array, a real citation), instructions, run(ctx) }`; `ctx` also has `pointer()` and `click()`; `ctx` (see `console.js`) gives a 960×540 stimulus canvas at 36 px/deg, frame timing, key/button responses, numeric entry, game-style result plots and CSV export; `fit.js` has the logistic MLE, linear and power fits. Every finished run's CSV is also kept on the device (`results.js`, localStorage) and listed on the MY RESULTS shelf (save one, SAVE ALL as a zip via `zip.js`, delete, clear). Files leave through `save.js` (`saveFile`): a download in a browser, the native bridge in the apps (iOS share sheet, Mac save panel). Lab versions of experiments (PsychoPy `.py`, Psychtoolbox-3 `.m`) live in `game/experiments/` and are listed in `scripts.js` (`SCRIPTED`); listed modules get export buttons on their instruction and results screens. **Visual-language exception:** the console's chrome follows the line rules, but the stimuli on its screen are real stimuli (Gabors, grey levels, coloured letters, dot fields) drawn on a canvas.
- **The coda** (`story/coda.js`): two LED-panel screens (title.js `LED`) inside one CRT session (`api.crt()`: opens on the white dot, `set`, `click`, `close` back down to the dot): the 1977 letter, then the postscript blinking on; then a second of silence and the credits, which end with the megaZINE masthead rolling up as part of the scroll and parking in the middle (`megazineRoll` in megazine.js) while the last lines roll away.
- `js/music.js`: the adaptive WebAudio score. One D-minor theme in a 40-bar form (A, A', B bridge, A, breath); `music.scene({ act, mode, room, flags })` picks a band per act and time of day (the OP-1 pluck to open the game and through Act I, building from a dry start; the Rhodes, MEGAjam's Tine engine, in Act II; then angular, restless, march; music box, vibes, bells; hazy mornings), room flavours (kitchen, library, MRI, server, underground, booth), and the driving finale in Act V underground; `music.endTheme()` scores the credits (Act I's sparse opening for 8 bars, then the finale built up in 8-bar layers as the tempo walks from 104 to 128, ending at full climax). Every on-beat or sustained note is a chord tone (`music.trace()` checks it); `music.renderOffline` renders previews. `js/guide.js` + `js/walkthrough.js`: the G U V walkthrough sheet (steps carry the flag that ticks them; keep it in step with puzzle changes). `js/nudge.js` reads two more step fields for stuck players: `hs` (the target `"room.id"` first, then the exits toward it; the first in the current room gets a stepped glint after ~35 s without progress) and `nudge` (2–3 in-voice `"WHO|line"`s, said one at a time from ~95 s, getting plainer). Non-obvious solutions need setups in what people say (an NPC names the need, a look-text or a second character connects the item), not just a nudge. Room changes run `swipe()` in main.js (stepped, ten poses).
- **Options, holidays, codes.** The HUD's OPTIONS chip (or O) opens `js/menu.js`: holiday decorations, hints (nudges), sound, fullscreen, and a cheat-code field. Settings and codes live in `js/options.js` (localStorage; each code that's on is a body class `c-<id>`). `js/holidays.js` decorates rooms for whatever's on the real calendar (moving feasts are looked up; `?holiday=winter` or `?date=2026-12-20` to test): a pennant banner, a garland, wall cutouts, ceiling hangings, desk props, placed per room in `ROOMS`; never underground. Codes are cosmetic only, except ALLACTS (every act open on the title screen; normally an act opens once you've reached it, `gaincontrol.act` in localStorage, and the rest are dim and unclickable): heads (every head is wrapped in `<g class="hd">`, so wrap new heads the same way), cicadas in Seminar 101, indoor snow, colour shifts, confetti, helium voices, TIMEWARP (steps the holidays) and GUV (the walkthrough). `js/whimsy.js` draws and plays them; `js/remarks.js` holds what people say about them (and the lines that let codes slip), said now and then on entering a room.
- **The kitchen regulars and darts** (`story/lounge.js`, `cast/lounge.js`, `minigames/darts.js`): some visits (rolled per room entry, `G.visit`), a sixth-year, a postdoc and/or a first-year lounge in the coffee kitchen (at night, sometimes a postdoc asleep on the beanbag). TALK alternates bad grad-school advice with rumors that hint at whatever the act still needs (`RUMORS[act][mode]`, keyed by unfinished flags; keep them in step with puzzle changes). The dartboard on the right wall (PLAY DARTS) is a casual game in any act: three rounds of three; the reticle sits exactly where you point, jittering only in proportion to the hidden spread; each opening (walk away and back: a new hand) rolls a hidden bias and Gaussian spread for your release, learnable only from the aim × marks and the postdoc's running estimate (mean miss ± SE in mm, spread) as N grows; best score in localStorage. Every act's kitchen spreads `LOUNGE_HS`.
- `preview.html?room=kitchen&mode=day&act=3&flags=a,b&hs=1` draws one room from flags, with hit areas and stand points marked (for art work).

## The Mac and iOS apps (`ios/`, `macos/`)
- Native shells around `game/`, copied from Townies' arrangement: the build copies `game/` into the app (`Web/`) and `LocalServer.swift` serves it on 127.0.0.1:47360 (fixed: the origin is where localStorage progress lives). iOS: `ios/GainControl.xcodeproj` (SwiftUI + WKWebView, landscape only, file-system-synchronized group, so new Swift files need no project edits). Mac: `macos/main.swift` + `macos/build.sh` (swiftc, no Xcode project), dmg published by `.github/workflows/macos.yml`; `ios.yml` compile-checks and screenshots the simulators.
- The page learns it's in an app from `window.GC_APP` (`"ios"` / `"mac"`) and the `gaincontrol` message handler; `js/native.js` holds everything app-specific (iOS haptics on the effects in `BUZZ`, kept in step with `GameBridge.swift`; no pinch zoom; errors to Xcode's console). On iOS, FULLSCREEN is hidden (`FULL.standalone()`); on the Mac the Fullscreen API takes the window full screen.
- Everything must keep working offline and by touch: no fonts or scripts from the web (Saira Condensed is bundled in `fonts/`), every key has a pointer equivalent, no hover-only information, and links out use `location.href` or `target=_blank` (the shells open them in the browser).

## Repo conventions
- Plain HTML, CSS and JS; no build step. Prototypes are single self-contained `index.html` files; the game is `game/` with ES modules.
- Commit and push work as it's finished.
- **Version number.** `game/js/version.js` holds a build number, shown in the title screen's lower right corner (V153, …). Bump it by one with every commit that changes the game, and tell the user the new number when reporting the change.
