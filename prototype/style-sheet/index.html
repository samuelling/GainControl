1. Two line weights. Drawn at a native 320×200, then scaled up: a 1.5 px line for objects and a 0.75 px hairline for grids, guides and interior detail (folds, seams, muscles, gears).
  2. Almost no fill. Closed shapes are filled black so lines overlap cleanly. Color fills are for things that are on (an LED, the insignia, a pupil, a lit cube) and for solid status chips with black lettering, like RECORDING.
  3. Color = material. Hair, cloth, skin, metal and wood each get one color. Skin is a pink line, never a filled face.
  4. Open contours. Faces are a jaw, a hairline and a few marks. Lines don't have to close.
  5. Poses, not tweens. Game & Watch / Pocket Operator logic: every frame is a pre-drawn pose that switches on and off in place. Toggle LCD GHOSTS to see the unlit segments.
  6. Motion as glyphs. Vibration ((( ))), speed dashes, sparks and drips are drawn marks, not effects.
  7. 3D as wireframe. Exploded isometric diagrams for close-ups, isometric cubes for maps (the Q*bert lineage), perspective grids for corridors.
  8. Condensed monoline type. Big, thin, rounded numbers carry the screen; tiny labels sit above them. All lettering is drawn as strokes at the same weight as the art.
