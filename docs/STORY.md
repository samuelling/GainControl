# GAIN CONTROL — Story Bible

> *A neuroscience murder mystery. The paper had no control condition. Now someone's trying to gain control.*

**Genre:** point-and-click comedy adventure
**Lineage:** Sierra structure and death screens (Laura Bow murder-mystery clock, Freddy Pharkas "day job" puzzles, Al Lowe narrator and puns), plus LucasArts flavor (Sam & Max deadpan duo, Day of the Tentacle character switching and cartoon logic), plus Gabriel Knight investigation (day structure, tape recorder, sketchbook, cipher, disguises)
**Look:** thin, uniform line art on black with almost no fill and pose-switching animation, in the spirit of the OP-1, Pocket Operators, Game & Watch and Q*bert (see Visual direction)
**Platform:** web (canvas) for now
**Rating target:** Al Lowe PG-13 — puns, innuendo, gross-out, cartoon murder

## The premise in one breath

Nell Null is a fifth-year grad student whose dissertation keeps coming up null. Across the hall, third-year golden boy Preston "P." Valu just published a dazzling 7T laminar fMRI paper in *Nurture Neuroscience*. The building has a 3T. As Nell starts asking questions, people who know too much start dying, Nell gets framed, and her only witness is an escaped lab rat that only she can hear. Her thesis defense is on Friday.

## The secret (what really happened)

1. Last spring Nell submitted her pilot paper (3T data, a top-down attention task: a strong visual response and an honest **null** for attention in V1). Nell scanned herself as pilot subject #1, retainer and all.
2. The journal sent it to famous Prof. Maximilian Impact for review. Impact, as usual, handed it to his student Preston to ghost-review.
3. Preston, writing as **Reviewer 2**, rejected it for being null ("the authors should simply collect more data"). Then he downloaded Nell's shared dataset.
4. He ran it through an AI super-resolution model on the department GPU cluster to upscale 3T into "7T laminar" data. The model hallucinated beautiful cortical layers everywhere, **including inside Nell's retainer artifact**.
5. He published it with fake participants, a backdated IRB protocol and imaginary scanner sessions. The job offers rolled in.
6. Now anyone who could connect the dots has to go. Preston kills the way he publishes: efficiently, and while framing someone else.

**So the answer to "AI or fabrication?" is both.** The data is real, but it's Nell's. The fake part is everything the AI added. And the words are fake too (see step 12): Preston hasn't written a paper himself in years.

### The deeper secret: the Neuroscientific Round Table (seeded from Act I, revealed at the Act III midpoint)
7. Preston didn't come up with this on his own. He's the newest recruit of the **Neuroscientific Round Table (NRT)**, a secret society of under-40 neuroscientists. For decades it has quietly steered the field: members review each other's papers kindly, sit on each other's grant panels, and play **Reviewer 2** to everyone else. Reviewer 2 isn't a person. It's an institution.
8. **The Schism of 1974.** The NRT began as the breakaway faction of an older round table, never named in the game. That older table was already morally loose: secret officers, members drawn from a few elite departments, a little mutual citation here, a friendly grant panel there. In 1974 a handful of defectors, led by a young Iris Candor, came back and confronted it at an open meeting. Under pressure, the old table reformed, at least on the surface: its officers became public and it admitted women. For the hardliners that was unforgivable. They wanted a society for **endless research**: endless funding, endless publications, endless careers, with nobody ever aging out and no result ever disproven. So they walked out, took the old table's archives and its B3 meeting room with them, and went underground for good. Members still toast "the Schism" at every induction, and they've never forgiven Iris Candor.
9. The NRT's real weapon is the **Gain Controller**: brain-stimulation tech that turns a person's neural gain up or down, so ambition goes up and conscience goes down. Preston's ever-present wireless earbuds are the receivers. He signed up willingly for the career boost, but the murders were the NRT's orders, carried out through him. **Preston is both villain and victim.** (This is where the title pays off.)
10. The NRT has met for decades in a sealed sub-basement under Fechner Hall, **B3**, around their "Round Table": a decommissioned 1960s research magnet, a giant iron ring. The building's pneumatic tube system was built in the 1960s for the old table, and the NRT inherited it. It's how they've passed reviews, grant scores and orders for half a century.
11. The leader, **the Corresponding Author**, is Prof. Quorum. His "death" at the faculty meeting was faked; nobody checked his pulse for 40 minutes. The society's under-40 rule is absolute, which is why Quorum has been officially 39 since 1979. His birth certificate is the oldest fabrication in the department.
12. **The paper mill.** Preston is the NRT's ghostwriter, and the ghost is a machine. The Archive in B3 holds fifty years of members' papers, reviews and grant text. Every Friday at midnight, on the department GPU cluster, Preston runs **the Large Table Model (LTM)**: a language model trained on that whole corpus. It writes new papers that read exactly like the Table's work because they're stitched from it. They're **metamers** of real science: different underneath, indistinguishable at a glance. Every one of Preston's 31 first-author papers in three years came out of the mill, and its queue has bylines for half the Rising Stars wall. The Big Finding was different only because the mill finally had real data to dress up: Nell's, upscaled. The Gain Controller supplies the will; the LTM supplies the words. It's the NRT's "endless research" made literal: research without researchers.
    - **The one thing Preston wrote himself was the rejection.** Reviewer 2's report on Nell's paper is full of human typos ("nueron," "utilize"). The LTM never misspells anything. The typos are Preston's fingerprints, and the rejection was personal.

### How the NRT works
*Loosely inspired by Charles Gross's 1977 letter in* American Psychologist *("Psychological Round Table in the 1960s"), a first-hand account of a real secret society of young experimental psychologists. We borrow its structure (secret officers, secret selection, the age cutoff, uncritical meetings, the archive) and push it much further toward the sinister. We deliberately leave out the letter's sexist specifics. Nothing here depicts a real organization or real people.*

- **Secret, self-perpetuating rule.** Six inner members, officially **the Editorial Board** and privately **"the Secret Six,"** choose their own successors and answer to no one. Ordinary members don't know who they are. Quorum heads it as Corresponding Author. Members never vote on anything.
- **The rule of silence.** Never put the NRT on your CV. Never claim it as an expense. Never tell anyone, including your partner, where you're going on a meeting night. This makes members findable by what's *missing*: the same blank calendar block, the same unexplained gap in every expense report.
- **Feeder labs.** Members come overwhelmingly from a few elite departments and labs, the same handful of names on every Rising Stars wall. Anyone who doesn't "fit in" never gets invited back, and nobody tells them why.
- **No criticism, ever.** Inside the Table, a member's work is never seriously questioned. Outside it, members are relentless. Kind review for the in-group and Reviewer 2 for everyone else.
- **The Archive and the long game.** The society keeps extensive records: membership rolls, every review, every grant score. The point is that members go on to sit on grant panels, editorial boards and hiring committees, where they keep favoring each other for the rest of their careers. Cross-reference the membership rolls with fifty years of panel rosters and you can see the whole field being steered. That cross-reference is Nell's final, damning piece of evidence.
- **The invitation.** Recruits get a warm, jokey letter on cream paper. It says the Table was founded by rebels against the Establishment (it is now the Establishment). It promises collegial irreverence, a Friday dinner, wine and "evening entertainment". Each new member must give a **Revelation**: ten minutes of unpublished work, read to the people who will later review it. There are no minutes and no program, and membership never goes on a CV. Members retire at forty, "the empirically determined age" for quiet agreement. A receipt can be provided calling it an "invited talk" for reimbursement. The after-dinner slides "must now be clean. Mostly." To accept, you reply with your **date of birth**, so they can make sure you aren't "wizened." It reads like fun. That's the point. Morris finds Preston's in Act III; Iris kept hers; Rex sent his DOB and got one word back. *(The NRT and its letter are fictional; the tone is drawn from the kind of age-gated, off-the-record invitations that still circulate.)*
- **Selection without consent.** You're never told you're a candidate. The Board watches you for years and then sets you a test: a single line to cross. **Preston's initiation test was Nell's paper.** The Board had Impact route the review to Preston, and stealing the data was how he proved himself.
- **Retirement at 40.** It used to mean leaving the club. In the NRT it means your gain is turned to zero. Retired members stay on as faculty: they sit through meetings blank-eyed, nod at every motion and say nothing new for the rest of their careers. *That explains a lot of faculty meetings.* Impact turned 41, tried to talk instead of retiring quietly, and was "retired" permanently. Quorum dodged it by forging his age.
- **The Hunt.** Every year the society chooses an outsider as its **quarry**: someone promising and unconnected. Then it closes ranks with Reviewer 2 reports, triaged grants and job searches that go cold. **This year's quarry is Nell Null.** Her paper's rejection was the opening move, not bad luck.
- **The mill.** Members don't just review each other kindly; since the LTM, many don't write at all. The Archive was always the Table's memory. Now it's also its training data. (LTM is also neuroscience shorthand for long-term memory, which members find hilarious.)
- **Fog neuro.** The Table's private name for its discipline of endless research: cognitive neuroscience as seen from a distance, with nothing solid inside. It never settles a question, because a settled question stops producing papers. Preston's CV covers every Big Question in the textbook, often in the same month. Under the Gain Controller he says the word out loud ("fog neuro… Cog. Cog neuro."), from his first line in Act I on.
- **Straw men and staged feuds.** Members stay prominent by fighting each other in public. The mill writes a hypothesis nobody holds, one Rising Star knocks it down, another "defends" it, and the field watches. Twice the citations for both sides. Preston's three-year feud with **Dr. Polly Mick** was written, both sides, by the same machine on the same nights.
- **Brilliant on paper, banal in person.** For all the secrecy, their actual meetings are intellectually empty: rote boilerplate, recycled slides, juvenile after-dinner roasts (which Morris loves). The NRT doesn't produce discoveries. It produces *success* and hands it out to its own. That's the game's theme again: the too-clean results come from people with nothing real to show.
- **The verdict: tragic, and pathetic.** Whatever the NRT has really done to grants, papers and jobs (and some of it is true), the game's last word on it is dismissive. Even if none of that were true, this would be: it's a lame attempt at a frat or a sorority, for people who should be above that sort of nonsense but who were left out socially their whole lives, and can't say no when the cream-paper letter finally comes. Write the members that way: not masterminds, joiners. When the 3T lands, the hood clasps are stuck to the ring and the hoods slide off: a study-section regular, an associate dean, the man who reviewed Nell's fellowship, blinking in bathrobes. Iris says it out loud ("A sorority. For people who… never got the letter." / "It's sad. It's also pathetic. You're allowed both, dear."), a member asks whether there's still dinner on Friday, and the aftermath has twelve robes in the B1 lost and found, unclaimed. INDUCTED gets it too: the Friday dinner is the back room of a chain restaurant, the same eleven people, the same 1998 reviewer story, and nobody has anywhere else to be.
- **The gavel.** The Board calls each induction to order with a brass gavel whose head is cast from the skull of the first member who turned 40 and refused to retire. The *old* table's gavel came with them in the Schism and was never used again: it's still in B3, in a canister wedged in the Tube Hub's DEAD LETTERS box, tagged THE OLD TABLE · DO NOT DELIVER · 1974, wrapped in a 1974 sports section with a card reading SPOILS OF THE SCHISM · NOT FOR CEREMONIAL USE. It's brass, and it is shaped like a gavel only in the loosest sense. Morris can find it in Act V (optional, +2); it's the in-world setup for the coda's postscript. It's described, never drawn.
- **Defectors.** A few people have walked out, but most never managed to get anyone to listen, and almost none got away. The one survivor is **Prof. Emerita Iris Candor** (see Cast).

## Theme

Real data is noisy and fake data is too clean, and the same goes for alibis. Every clue in the game comes down to *noticing something too perfect*. Preston's alibis are spotless, his figures have no motion artifacts, and his participants never miss an appointment.

## Setting: Fechner Hall, Charles River University

A fictional university building, 1960s brutalist with a 2020s "innovation atrium" bolted on.

- **Basement:** MRI suite ("The Big Donut", 3T), control room, Tess's office, animal facility with the water maze, cold room, liquid nitrogen fill station, -80 freezer room, GPU server room, the MRI equipment room (where the scanner's chilled-water pipes run into the steam tunnels).
- **1st floor:** innovation atrium with the "Rising Stars" photo wall (Preston's photo is twice as big as anyone's), seminar room (journal club, pizza), main office (Penny), mailroom, coffee kitchen with a broken espresso machine.
- **2nd floor:** Adline Lab (dark psychophysics booths, a cluttered desk with Morris's drawer), Impact Lab (gleaming, empty, and suspiciously free of equipment), the shared printer (always jammed).
- **3rd floor:** faculty offices (Impact, Adline, Quorum), the conference room, Ethel Bord's integrity office.
- **Throughout:** a 1960s **pneumatic mail tube** system that still works. This is the Chron-o-John: Nell and Morris use it to pass items between floors. Some canisters go to an address that isn't on any floor: **B3**.
- **Steam tunnels:** reached through the MRI equipment room. They run under the whole campus and are mostly a maze of pipes, with one bricked-over door marked with the NRT insignia (a circle seated around a smaller circle, like a round table).
- **B3, the sealed sub-basement:** the old 1960s Psychophysics Annex, "closed after the fire of '71." Inside:
  - an antechamber with a robe rack and portraits of members, all aged 39
  - the **Tube Hub**, where every pneumatic line in the building ends
  - the **Archive**: half a century of secret reviews and grant scores on reel-to-reel magnetic tape
  - **the Round Table Chamber**, built around the giant decommissioned magnet ring
  - the old power plant room that feeds the magnet

## Story structure: Defense Week

### The Hot Fuzz principle
Gain Control is a comedy with a secret society living underneath it. The model is *Hot Fuzz*: a ridiculous place full of ridiculous people, deaths that look like silly accidents, jokes that never stop, and then the robed society that's been running everything all along. Afterward, every earlier joke turns out to have been a clue.

**Rule for every seed:** it plays as a joke the first time and as horror on the second look. Academia's absurdity is the cover story, and the Table is what it's covering.

### Two tones: day and night
- **Day shifts (Nell):** Al Lowe and Sam & Max. Bright palette, crowded hallways, office hours, the broken espresso machine, full-volume narrator.
- **Night shifts (Gus):** Gabriel Knight. A few cool colors on a lot of black, empty corridors, the scanner humming at 2 AM, canisters rattling in the walls, a robe left on a seminar chair. The narrator gets quieter at night: drier, fewer gags, more pauses.
- The darkness rises through the night shifts while the days stay funny. In Act V the two tones **merge**: Nell's day ends underground at midnight, and all three characters share one palette.

### Five layers, five acts
Each act peels back one layer of the mystery. The acts are named for the stages of a manuscript, because Nell's whole week is one long peer review.

| Act | Day | Title | Question | Answer at act's end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Mon | **Submission** | Is the paper fake? | Almost certainly, and someone's warning Nell off |
| II | Tue | **Under Review** | Who's killing people? | Apparently Preston, and Nell is framed |
| III | Wed | **Major Revisions** | Why? | It's not one cheater, it's a system, and Nell is this year's quarry (**midpoint reversal**) |
| IV | Thu | **Rebuttal** | Who's pulling Preston's strings? | The Table, through the Gain Controller. Preston is a puppet |
| V | Fri | **Decision** | Can they be stopped? | That depends on you |

### Death cadence
One or two per act, each a victim the **Table** needed silenced, not just someone in Preston's way.

| Act | Victim | Why the Table wanted them gone |
|---|---|---|
| II | Tess La | Knew no 7T session ever happened (she'd seen Preston in the building four times in three years, always at pizza), had noticed the midnight Friday power draw on the old B-level circuit, and, once Nell saw the calendar, said out loud that something was off: she'd raised it with the Chair at faculty lunch, and announced she'd pull the console's raw logs that night for Ethel. The console was wiped at 2:14 AM |
| III | Dana Outlier | Told Nell about Preston's hours, and had started asking where he went on Friday nights |
| III | Prof. Quorum (faked) | Clears the leader off the board right as suspicion builds |
| IV | Cody Credit | Decided to tell Det. Small about the fake sign-in sheet |
| IV | Prof. Impact | Turned 41 and tried to talk instead of retiring quietly |
| IV (night) | Gus Ian | Found the B3 map and the induction date, and told Nell. The Board turned his gain to zero |

### Act I — Monday: SUBMISSION
*Playable vertical slice in `game/`; room-by-room design and puzzle dependencies in [ACT1.md](ACT1.md).*

**Question:** Is the paper fake?

**Day (Nell):** Journal club. Preston presents his dazzling paper to applause and free pizza. Nell looks at the methods slide and asks, "Wait, when did we get a 7T?" The room goes quiet and Preston's smile freezes. Morris, in the ceiling tiles for the pizza, hears Preston mutter something on his way out.
- **Teaches:** verbs, Nell/Morris switching, the pneumatic tube, the voice memo. Nell records Preston saying *"I ran every subject myself."*
- **Office hours:** the first science lesson. "Tesla is how strong the magnet is…"

**Seeds (each plays as a joke):**
- **Iris Candor** sits in the back row and asks, *"And who reviewed it?"* She gets shushed. Narrator: "Nobody knows who she is. She has attended journal club since 1961."
- **Quorum's cufflinks** carry the insignia. Look: *"Tasteful. Round. Expensive."*
- **The senior faculty** nod in perfect unison. Narrator: *"Tenure does that to a person."*
- **Preston's earbuds.** He never takes them out, not even to eat pizza.
- **Morris** gets an itch whenever he's near Preston. *"Fleas. Probably."*
- **A canister** zips past in the tube, addressed **B3**. Nell: "There's no B3."
- **A hooded figure** glimpsed at the edge of the atrium. (Reviewer 2. Everyone has one.)

**Night (Gus):** the tutorial for Gus's shift. Gus Ian, Nell's labmate, arrives for his 2 AM scan (the 3T is cheapest after 11pm): badge, phone, energy drink. In Seminar 101 he finds a hooded robe draped over a chair, its small round pin lit red, and photographs it. *"Halloween's early this year."* Canisters keep rattling *down*, past the basement.

**Turn:** a sticky note on Nell's monitor holds nothing but a string of brain coordinates. Decoded on Tuesday morning (the first cipher lesson): *"CURIOSITY KILLED THE CAT. ALSO GRAD STUDENTS."*

**Emotional beat:** Nell is alone. Her results are null, her PI is unavailable until after the 15th, and her one labmate only exists after midnight. The only one on her side in daylight is a rat.

### Act II — Tuesday: UNDER REVIEW
**Question:** Who's killing people?

**Day (Nell):** the paper trail.
- The scanner booking calendar shows Preston never booked a session (Tess's office, via Morris's helium-alarm trick).
- No 7T or laminar coil was ever purchased (Penny's purchase orders, via Form PO-7).
- The IRB protocol was approved *after* the data were collected (Ethel's binder of binders).
- The participant sign-in sheet includes *H. Molaison, P. Gage, S. M.* and *Totally Real Person* (Cody hands over the clipboard after hearing Preston's recorded lie).

**Seeds:**
- Penny complains that everyone important is "unreachable on Friday nights. Every Friday. For decades."
- Tess grumbles that something keeps drawing power from the old B-level circuit at midnight on Fridays.
- **Tess turns.** Once Nell has seen the calendar, Tess stops guarding it and starts agreeing: she has seen Preston in the building four times in three years ("all four, there was pizza"), her 3T "doesn't do layers," she mentioned it to the Chair at faculty lunch (he said "interesting" and wrote it down), and tonight she's pulling the raw console logs, alone, for Ethel. Canisters drop toward the basement as she says it. That night Gus sees a robe carrying a steel thermos toward the only lit room in B1.
- Rex's first rant: *"This whole field is rigged. I could tell you things."* (Nobody asks.)

**Night (Gus):** Gus works out the Impact Lab's keypad code from its four worn keys and finds Preston's computer still logged in, running **FOG NEURO**: the mill's front end, which writes a new fake paper on a different Big Question of cognitive neuroscience every time you press GENERATE. It's a playable toy (an in-game METAMERS; design in [PUZZLE-METAMERS.md](PUZZLE-METAMERS.md)). Gus prints one because "it looked important." Then, at 2am, two robed figures cross the MRI hallway. Gus follows, gets called back by a gradient fault on his scan (he's paying by the hour), and arrives five minutes late.

**Death:** Tess La, found in the magnet room in the morning, killed by a flying ferromagnetic thermos labeled **NELL**.

**Turn:** Nell is the prime suspect and Det. Sgt. Small arrives. But for the first time, the two shifts cross: Gus, still at his desk at 9 AM, believes her. *"Wasn't you. Saw robes."* He hands her the printout. *"Also this. Nell, this is foul play."* From here on they work the case together.

**Emotional beat:** fear. Morris's loyalty: *"If you go down, pal, I'm going down the drain with you."*

### Act III — Wednesday: MAJOR REVISIONS (the midpoint)
*Playable: room-by-room design and build spec in [ACT3.md](ACT3.md). The build keeps the four proofs, Dana, THE NOD, Quorum, Iris and the tunnels; the metamer overlay, the insignia tracing, the missing hours and the library move to Act IV.*

**Question:** Why?

**Day (Nell):** Nell investigates under suspicion, interrupted by Det. Small's interrogations. Gus, running on no sleep, is her methods partner at the desk.
- **The fraud, proven:** Table 1's brain-behaviour correlations sit above the noise ceiling, because he picked the voxels that correlated (THE CEILING), Figure 3b and 3d are the same panel flipped (overlay), and 4,000 GPU-hours on `superres_laminar.py` (Rex's logs, bought with coffee), plus a second job that runs every Friday at 00:00: `ltm_generate.py --corpus /mnt/B3/archive --byline "P. Valu"`. Its output queue holds 40 finished drafts with future dates, including *Valu et al. (2027): "The Null Result of N. Null Is an Artifact."*
- **The replication** (centerpiece; full design in [PUZZLE-GLM.md](PUZZLE-GLM.md)): with Preston's own stimulus log (Morris, from the Impact Lab) and his deposited raw data, Nell and Gus run the GLM by hand at Nell's monitor. Attention does turn V1 up (ATTEND > IGNORE, as everyone has known since the 1990s), but at 3T a single voxel is bigger than all six layers, so the effect is the same in every "layer". Preston's claim was never the effect; it was where it lives. *"Attention's real. His layers aren't."* / *"So the fancy layers?"* / *"Painted on."*
- **The conspiracy, surfacing:**
  - **Preston's blank moments.** Morris's itch spikes, Preston's earbuds pulse, and his face goes empty mid-sentence.
  - **The insignia,** traced in the notebook: earbud case, sticky notes, Quorum's cufflinks, the brass plate on the tube hub. They all match.
  - **Rex's legend:** "There's a secret society. Under forty. I applied at 39 and 11 months. They said my CV was *too old*."
  - **The metamer:** Nell overlays the Big Finding's sentences on the library's back issues. Gus's FOG NEURO printout taught her what to look for. Every sentence comes from a different older paper, and every source paper's authors come from the same feeder labs. The paper is a collage of the Table's own work, and its author list is a partial membership roll. Some references don't exist at all (Quorum, 1979b; *J. Endless Res.*).
  - **The missing hours:** Preston, Impact and Quorum all share the same Friday-night expense gap, and the same three feeder labs on their CVs.
  - **The old notes:** some threats don't decode with the modern atlas. In the library, hunting for the 1980s atlas, Nell finds a bookmarked 1977 letter signed *I. Candor*.

**Deaths:**
- **Dana Outlier**, found "excluded" in the recycling.
- **Prof. Quorum** "dies" at the faculty meeting. The meeting continues for 40 minutes; the senior faculty keep nodding. This is the comic-horror peak: the funniest scene in the game, and the most sinister on a second look. Before he dies, Nell and Gus crack *why* they nod: the faculty are answering the NRT's ritual, a 2-back over the motions (**THE NOD**; see [PUZZLE-NBACK.md](PUZZLE-NBACK.md)).

**Day's last scene:** Nell finds Iris in her forgotten emeritus office (she never goes home). She explains the Secret Six, the rule of silence, retirement at 40 and the Hunt. Then:
> *"You're this year's quarry, dear. Your paper didn't get rejected. You got selected."*

**Night (Gus):** Gus follows a canister's rattle through the MRI equipment room into the steam tunnels and finds the bricked door with the insignia. Through it, chanting in peer-review boilerplate: *"The authors should consider… the authors should consider…"* The canister he catches holds one line:

### Act V — Friday: DECISION
*Playable: build spec in [ACT5.md](ACT5.md).*

Day and night merge. The finale comes in two halves: Laura Bow's courtroom, then Gabriel Knight's lair.

Morris loosens them, bolt by bolt, without dropping it early.

**The climax:**
1. The Editorial Board calls the induction to order with the skull gavel. Quorum unhoods. He's not dead, he's 39, and he makes the Hunt's traditional final offer to its quarry: join instead of being destroyed. *"Think of your funding odds."* Saying yes is a valid (bad) ending.
2. **Iris arrives** up the service stair while the supply charges. Fifty years late and right on time: *"I told you in 1974."* She staples Quorum's robe to the Round Table, which buys Morris the time he needs.
3. **The archive (required for the best ending):** before the magnet comes on, Morris sends the most damning reels up the pneumatic tube to Ethel Bord's office. The tube system the Table built becomes the thing that exposes it.
4. **The magnet:** Iris throws the operator's knife switch beside the ring (*"I commissioned this magnet in 1968. I've waited a long time to turn it back on."*) and the Round Table ramps to full field. Every hood clasp, cufflink and earbud in the room flies to the ring. Preston's earbuds go, setting him free. Then the ring finds the other magnet: the 3T, two floors up, always on, on a pad held up by nothing but habit. ## Gameplay pillars

### 1. Sierra deaths with a LucasArts safety net
Every death gets an Al Lowe narrator joke, but the death screen has two buttons:
- **Revise & Resubmit:** rewinds to just before the death (LucasArts-friendly, so no lost progress).
- **Withdraw Manuscript:** quit.

This way you get Sierra's death jokes without its punishment.

### 1b. The chooser (our take on Gabriel Knight's context interface)
Clicking the floor walks. Clicking anything interactive opens a **chooser** right there: a reticle, the object's name chip, and an arc of glyph buttons showing only the actions that make sense, with object-specific labels (TAKE SLICE, PHOTOGRAPH, SHAKE HAND, OPEN HATCH). Inventory items get the same chooser (LOOK, USE ON, and item verbs like PLAY on the voice memo recorder). Walking stays effortless, and every interactive thing feels deliberate.

### 2. Three-character switching (the Day of the Tentacle engine)
Day of the Tentacle split its heroes across time. Here they split across **day and night**.

- **Nell (day shift)** talks to people, uses doors, signs forms and operates equipment. People take her semi-seriously.
- **Gus (night shift, Acts I–IV)** has a scanner badge, a 2 AM scan slot, a laptop, and every analysis trick Nell doesn't know. His badge opens the basement, the MRI suite and anything a grad student can get into at night (not faculty offices), his phone camera documents what he finds, and he sees who is in the building at 3am. Nobody notices a grad student at 2 AM. Grad students are furniture. In Act V, Iris takes the third route.
- **Morris (both shifts)** is a rat, so he's nocturnal and also awake whenever there's food. He goes through vents, ceilings and walls, steals small things, eavesdrops and causes chaos. He's the link between the two shifts.

**Passing things across shifts:**
- **The pneumatic tube** moves items between floors in the same shift.
- **The shared lab desk** (and the lab's shared drive) moves items between shifts. Nell leaves something on Gus's desk at 5pm; he finds it at 2am. Gus leaves a sticky note or a half-finished script at 3am; Nell finds it at 9am.
- **Cause and effect across shifts:** what Gus does at night changes the next day. If he props the basement stairwell door with a chair, it's still propped in the morning. If he leaves the scanner log open, Nell can read who scanned what.

**When you can play Gus:** each of Acts I–IV ends with a night shift (Monday night through Thursday night) before the next day's title card. The night shifts are when the murders get set up, and Gus is often a few minutes too late. On Thursday night, he isn't too late; that's the problem.

**Example puzzle:** to get into Tess's office, Morris goes into the vent above the scanner control room and chews a wire, which sets off the "helium low" alarm. Tess runs out. Nell walks in.

### 3. Day job interludes (the Freddy Pharkas engine)
Nell is still a TA. Undergrads from her stats section show up at random with questions, just as Freddy's customers did. A correct answer earns points and sometimes an item or favor. The same stats skills (reliability, distributions, outliers) are what crack the case later, so the day job trains you for the mystery.

### 4. Sam & Max texture
- Use/Look on *everything* gets a joke, especially Morris's lines.
- Deadpan, surreal side characters, and running gags that pay off in later acts.
- Morris's "solutions" are frequently violent and must be talked down.

### 5. Investigation tools (the Gabriel Knight engine)
- **Days, not rooms.** As in *Sins of the Fathers*, each day opens with a title card ("MONDAY — JOURNAL CLUB"). A day ends only when you've done its key things, and the clock moves the cast around the building.
- **Voice memo recorder:** Gabriel's tape recorder. Record what people say and play it back to someone else for a reaction. Play Preston's "I ran every subject myself" to Cody; play Penny's "nobody ordered a coil" to Prof. Impact. Recording Morris gets you nothing but squeaks.
- **Lab notebook:** Gabriel's sketchbook. Trace figure panels, signatures and artifact shapes, then compare them over other images. This is how the duplicated panel and the retainer reveal work.
- **The coordinate cipher:** the voodoo code. The killer's threatening notes are strings of MNI brain coordinates. Look each one up in the atlas on Nell's computer, and the region names' initials spell out the message. Nell learns the code a few symbols at a time, and in Act V she writes her *own* note in it to lure Preston into the trap.
- **Disguises:** the cat-hair mustache tradition. Nell gets into the faculty-only lounge as Prof. Impact, using a keynote lanyard, an airport neck pillow, and Morris on her head as the hair.
- **Archive research:** Nell can read old papers, Preston's CV and the department newsletter archive to look up what she needs (who had scanner access in which years, what "laminar" actually requires). These are real reading puzzles, the way Gabriel researched at the bookstore and library.

### 6. Score = h-index
Points are "citations." The status bar reads *h-index: 14 of 50*.

### 7. Real neuroscience, explained simply
Every science puzzle uses a real concept, explained so a non-scientist can solve it. The rule is the same as Freddy Pharkas's real chemistry: **the game teaches you the idea right before it asks you to use it.** Nell's office-hours students, Rex's rants, or the notebook give you the plain-language version. No jargon is needed to solve anything, and a neuroscientist should be able to nod along.

## Visual direction: line, pose, segment
**Status: locked.** Canonical references: `prototype/room-101/` (a playable scene) and `prototype/style-sheet/` (Style Sheet 02), checked against the official OP-1 module screens. `CLAUDE.md` has the short-form rules for anyone building scenes. The look draws on the OP-1 and Pocket Operator screens, Game & Watch LCDs, and early-80s arcade games like Q*bert, but crisper. We borrow the drawing rules, not any product's actual graphics, UI layout, controls or characters.

### The eight rules
1. **Two line weights.** Everything is drawn at a native 320×200, then scaled up: a 1.5 px line for objects and a 0.75 px hairline for grids, guides and interior detail (folds, seams, muscles, gears).
2. **Almost no fill.** Closed shapes are filled black so lines overlap cleanly. Color fills are for things that are *on* (an LED, the NRT insignia, a canister, a lit cube, a pupil, Morris's implant) and for solid status chips with black lettering (RECORDING, NIGHT SHIFT).
3. **Color = material.** Hair, cloth, skin, metal and wood each get one color. Skin is a pink line, never a filled face.
4. **Open contours.** Faces are a jaw, a hairline and a few marks. Lines don't have to close.
5. **Poses, not tweens.** Game & Watch / Pocket Operator logic: every animation frame is a pre-drawn pose that switches on and off in place. Movement across the screen steps between fixed positions (the thermos flies in four poses, then CLANG). Optional "LCD ghosts" show unlit poses faintly.
6. **Motion as glyphs.** Vibration `((( )))`, speed dashes, sparks, drips and steam are drawn marks, not effects.
7. **3D as wireframe.** Exploded isometric diagrams for clue close-ups (Preston's earbud), isometric cubes for maps (the Q*bert lineage, the steps to B3), perspective grids for corridors (the steam tunnels).
8. **Condensed monoline type.** Big, thin, rounded numbers carry the screen, with tiny labels above them (DAY / MON, H-INDEX / 14). All lettering is drawn as strokes at the same weight as the art.

### Point-and-click specifics
- **Proportions:** everything in a room is sized against the walking characters (a chair back is about half a standing figure; a seated head matches a standing head).
- **Unimportant things stay simple:** anonymous audience members are just chair backs; only story-relevant people show a head and one identifying mark.
- **Dialogue type:** large condensed all-caps (Saira Condensed), each line in its speaker's color with a solid speaker chip. HUD and signage use the stroke alphabet.
- **Laminar fMRI on screen** is a close-up of folded cortex (pial surface, six layer lines following the fold, white-matter boundary, depth probe), never concentric rings.
- **Talking portraits (Gabriel Knight) are essential.** Close-ups drawn with the same rules at a larger native size, with swapped poses for blink, mouth and expression. Preston's blank-out happens in close-up.
- **Day vs. night:** day scenes use the full palette; Gus's night shifts drop to DEEP, COOL, ON and DIM, plus the red EXIT sign.
- **Hotspots** follow the no-fill rule: atmosphere is drawn in STRUCT/DIM, things that matter get full color, and a few colored objects are red herrings.
